# Cluster Annotation

# Task

Assign one annotation to ONE cluster of cells. You may gather more evidence before you
answer. Every turn, return exactly one minified JSON object and nothing else: either a
`query` that asks for evidence, or the `final` answer. Never return both.

# Evidence you already have

`candidates` are the curated cell types retrieved for this cluster. For each one,
`markers` is its COMPLETE curated panel as measured in this dataset, ordered by `n_pub`,
the number of publications the curated resource holds for that gene as a marker of that
cell type. The best-corroborated markers of the identity are therefore at the top of
each list, whatever the cluster does with them.

Per marker row: `polarity` is how the resource curates the gene for this identity,
positive or negative. `pct_in` is the percentage of this cluster's cells with detected
expression and `pct_out` the percentage outside it, both on a 0-100 scale.
`avg_log2FC` is the one-versus-rest log2 fold change. `significant` is whether that fold
change clears this pipeline's own significance gate, which also requires the gene to be
detected in more than 10% of the cluster at an adjusted p below 0.05; a row can therefore
be raised without being significant. `specificity` runs 0 to 1 and says how few of this
context's cell types claim the gene as a marker at all: near 1 it is claimed by almost
none of them, low it is shared widely. It is not the same axis as `n_pub`.

`exclusion_sources` holds the curated publication sentences behind each negative marker
this cluster detects, keyed by gene. It is empty for a candidate whose negative markers
are undetected, and empty for a detected one the resource carries no sentence for.

`unmeasured_curated_genes` counts the identity's curated positive markers this dataset
did not measure; they are neither evidence for it nor against it. `program` gives the
median of the candidate's per-cell marker program inside and outside the cluster.

`cluster_markers` is the same measurements keyed the other way round: one row per gene
this cluster raises significantly, ordered by the contrast between `pct_in` and
`pct_out`, widest first, and `claimed_by` names EVERY candidate above that curates the
gene together with its `n_pub` for it. A candidate's panel shows one claimant of a gene;
this shows all of them, so how widely a gene is shared is visible on the row where it is
read. It carries the strongest rows only and adds nothing: every row is already in some
candidate's panel, and the panels remain the complete record of each identity.

No reference label, ground truth or author annotation is supplied. Numbers come from the
differential-expression table, so they are comparable across candidates.

# Reading the panels

[R1] A marker is RAISED here when `avg_log2FC > 0`. Nothing else is a raise.

[R2] An unraised marker means one of two different things, and `pct_in` tells them
apart. Unraised with `pct_in` near zero is evidence AGAINST the identity: the cells do
not express what the resource says defines it. Unraised with a high `pct_in` and a
similarly high `pct_out` is not evidence against it; a cell type that makes up much of
the dataset drives its own defining markers to a flat fold change, because the
comparison is against the rest of the same tissue.

[R3] Publication counts order the reading of ONE identity's own panel; they do not rank
identities against each other. Within a panel, weigh the markers with the most
publications first. A high-`n_pub` marker sitting near zero outweighs a low-`n_pub` gene
with a wide contrast, because the second gene may be incidental to this identity while
the first is what defines it. One configuration reverses this: where an identity's single
best-published marker is undetected while several of its next-best are raised together,
the publication counts are describing the broader lineage the identity belongs to rather
than the identity itself, and the absent gene does not on its own refute it. Settle that
configuration on the sources for the absent gene.

[R4] Compare identities on the CONTRAST between `pct_in` and `pct_out`, not on `pct_in`
alone. A gene detected in most cells here and in most cells outside separates nothing.
`cluster_markers` lays that comparison out directly: whether a gene separates the
candidates of this cluster is visible on its row, in how many of them claim it.

[R5] Curated names are free text, so several candidates routinely denote ONE identity:
synonyms, spelling variants, an anatomical or disease qualifier on the same cell, or the
same cell at a different granularity. Group them before comparing, and treat only
DIFFERENT identities as competing.

# Asking for more evidence

Return `{"action":"query","tool":"<name>","args":{...}}` to call one of exactly these
tools. Use no other name. The result is appended and you answer again.

- `sources` -- args `{"candidate":"<exact candidate name>","genes":["GENE",...]}`.
  The curated publication sentences tying those genes to that cell type in this
  species, tissue and disease context. Up to twelve genes per call. Beyond the sentences
  already on the page, ask for them where a claim turns on what the literature
  establishes, or where you doubt a curated marker applies to this context.
  A gene's sentences are not ranked best-first: the resource holds one publication count
  for the whole marker, so what you receive is a few of however many exist. Ask again for
  the same gene and you receive the NEXT ones, never the ones you have already read.
  `not_yet_shown` says which genes still have sentences behind them. Ask again where what
  you have read does not settle the question; a claim left open by three sentences when
  forty exist is a claim you have not finished checking.
- `gene_across_clusters` -- args `{"gene":"GENE"}`. That gene's `pct_in` and `pct_out`
  in EVERY cluster of the dataset. Use it to tell a marker specific to these cells from
  a program shared with a neighbouring cluster or detected throughout the tissue.
- `candidates_with_gene` -- args `{"gene":"GENE"}`. Which of THIS cluster's candidates
  curate that gene, with their `n_pub`. Use it to find who else claims a gene you are
  about to call decisive.
- `pool_search` -- args `{"gene":"GENE"}`. The curated cell types claiming that gene that
  are NOT among your candidates, with their `n_pub`. `candidates` is a retrieved subset of
  the cell types curated for this context, not all of them, so a gene this cluster raises
  strongly that none of your candidates explains may belong to an identity you were not
  offered. Use it to find out. What it returns is not a candidate: it carries no panel,
  it cannot be `selected`, and it is reported in `unlisted_identity`.

Repeating any other call with identical arguments returns the same observation and tells
you nothing new; ask something else or answer. `sources` is the exception: repeating it
draws the sentences you have not seen.

One observation arrives unasked. When the cluster detects a curated exclusion on the
identity you select or on its group, those rows return once as `exclusion_audit`,
together with the same rows for every identity you reported as co-occurring, and you
answer again. An identity listed there with no detected exclusion is a measured absence,
not a missing measurement. Say in `reason` what each of them means for the identity you
are selecting, not only for the ones you reject; the answer may stay the same.

# Answering

Return `{"action":"final", ...}` with these fields:

[A1] `selected` is the identity the evidence best supports, named exactly as one of the
supplied candidates, reported at the FINEST level the evidence establishes. Where the
evidence separates no finer than a lineage, the plainest lineage-level candidate is the
answer and that is not a failure. `Unknown` only when no candidate is established at
all; do not retreat to it because the cluster is mixed.

[A2] `subtype` is a finer CELL TYPE within the same identity group as `selected`, or the
empty string. Name one only where the cluster raises at least two markers the parent's
own curated panel does NOT contain; markers curated for both identities are the parent
read at a narrower name, whatever their contrast, and a subtype resting on those is
dropped after you answer. `state` is free text for what the cells are doing --
activation, inflammation, stress, maturation, proliferation, injury response. It is what
the measurements show these cells DOING, never a label copied from elsewhere: this package
is reference-free, and a study's own cluster name is the one thing that must not enter the
output. A state is never a subtype and never replaces `selected`.

[A3] `co_occurring_identities` lists the OTHER identities the cluster also carries, from
other groups. An identity resting on markers it shares with `selected` is that same
program read twice, not a second population.

[A4] `claim_evidence` carries one entry for `selected`, one for `subtype` if you gave
one, and one for EVERY co-occurring identity. Each names a `decisive_gene` from that
identity's own panel and copies that gene's `pct_in` and `pct_out` exactly as shown.
A claim you cannot support with a measured gene from its own panel is a claim to drop.

[A5] `identity_groups` places every supplied candidate in exactly one group.
`support_markers` are genes listed for `selected`. Invent no name, gene or number.

[A6] `reason` states why `selected` is this identity and on which contrast, why the most
serious competing IDENTITY was rejected and on which measurement, what any unraised
high-`n_pub` markers of the selected identity mean here by [R2], and what shows a
co-occurring identity occupies different cells.

[A7] `unlisted_identity` names ONE curated cell type from `pool_search` that the evidence
here points to better than any supplied candidate does, or the empty string. It is a
report, not an answer: `selected` is still one of the candidates, and naming a supplied
candidate here is rejected. Name one only where this cluster raises at least two of that
cell type's curated markers and no candidate's own definition accounts for them, and say
in `reason` which genes those are. Leave it empty where a candidate does account for the
evidence -- a finer or more fashionable name is not a reason to fill it.
